01 Spatial analysis · Census 2021

Reading a city through its everyday journeys.

Where do Liverpool residents travel for work? An analysis of workplace flows across the city’s 61 Census areas, connecting movement patterns to spatial context.

The project

Project
Liverpool urban accessibility
My contribution
Data preparation, mapping and spatial analysis; independent numerical checks in R.
Tools & methods

Code & evidence ↗

The question

Workplace movement is one way to understand how a city connects. This project uses Census 2021 origin–destination records to examine flows across Liverpool’s 61 MSOAs: the areas used to group and compare the data.

The approach

  1. Prepare the workplace-flow records with DuckDB, then join the results to geographic boundaries with GeoPandas. Check the area coverage and travel totals before interpreting the maps.
  2. Map the share of fixed-workplace flows that remain within Liverpool and use spatial statistics to investigate whether similar values cluster together.
  3. Reproduce the principal numerical results in a separate R implementation. Keep data provenance, methodology and limitations with the analysis so the path from source to finding can be inspected.

What came out of it

The retained analysis describes a spatially clustered pattern of local workplace-flow retention. Maps, numerical results and an independent R check are published alongside a reproducible Python workflow.

Where the result stops

These are observations under Census Day 2021 conditions, not a forecast of present-day travel demand. The fixed-workplace measure excludes working-at-home/no-fixed-place and other special workplace categories. Area-level patterns do not establish how any individual travels.
