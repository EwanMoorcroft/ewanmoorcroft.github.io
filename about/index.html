ABOUT From Geography to machine learning

Curious about data.
Careful with conclusions.

I’m Ewan, an MSc Data Science and AI student at the University of Liverpool. My current work centres on machine-learning evaluation, 3D computer vision and forecasting. I’m looking for graduate and junior opportunities where I can keep developing those skills through useful, well-tested software.

Before this, I studied Geography. That background still shapes how I approach data: I pay attention to where measurements come from, the scale they describe and the conclusions they can support. My undergraduate field research on the River Soar examined riparian buffers, land use and nutrient conditions. Working with observations from real places made data quality and uncertainty practical concerns.

From measurements to models

The transition into data science gave me more ways to investigate those questions. In the Liverpool workplace-flow project, I used SQL and spatial tools to reduce a national table to a clear local question. Checking the numerical results independently in R helped separate an implementation check from the much harder question of whether the model adequately described the world.

My dissertation applies that same attention to a different kind of data: individual trees in UAV LiDAR point clouds. I integrated existing segmentation methods into a shared evaluation workflow. The architectures belong to their original authors; my contribution is making predictions comparable, preserving the identity of result routes and checking how the aggregate scores are reconstructed.

Forecasting adds another constraint: time. In my FPL project, a feature is only useful if it could have existed when the prediction was made. That led me to focus on snapshot validation, as-of features and chronological evaluation before interpreting differences between models. A small improvement over a recent-form baseline is a useful result when its limits are clear.

Practical work, inspectable results

I enjoy the part of a project where modelling meets software: defining accepted inputs, making failures understandable and giving someone else a way to inspect the output. My projects include command-line interfaces, automated checks and local packaging. I distinguish these from operating a public production service, which the portfolio does not claim.

Alongside my studies, I have worked in food production, markets and warehouse operations. At Fosse Meadows I worked in a two-person market team, serving customers, handling payments and preparing orders against same-day deadlines. These roles required clear communication and reliable follow-through, even when the work was very different from writing Python.

I want to bring that combination of technical curiosity and practical care to an early-career role. The case studies show the decisions I can explain today, including what each result leaves unresolved. My CV gives the shorter version, and I’m happy to discuss the implementation or the next improvement over email.
